Almost every student who comes to a typing class asks the same question in the first week: "Sir, typing speed kaise badhaye?" The honest answer is that there is no shortcut, but there is a smart path. Most people plateau not because they lack talent, but because they practise the wrong way, with the wrong posture, or without measuring anything.

This guide collects 15 tips that work for both English and Hindi typists, especially those preparing for government exams like SSC CHSL, SSC CGL DEST, RRB NTPC and CPCT. Follow them in order and you should see steady improvement within a few weeks.

## First, Know Where You Stand

Before you try to improve anything, measure it. Take a [5-minute typing test](/typing-test/5-minute/) and note three numbers: gross speed, net speed and accuracy. If you are not sure what these mean, read our guide on [what WPM is and how net speed is calculated](/blog/what-is-wpm-typing-speed/).

Here is a rough benchmark for exam aspirants:

| Your current net speed | Where you are | Realistic target in 6-8 weeks |
|---|---|---|
| Below 20 WPM | Beginner, often looking at keys | 25-30 WPM |
| 20-30 WPM | Developing touch typist | 35-40 WPM |
| 30-40 WPM | Exam-ready for most typist posts | 40-50 WPM |
| Above 40 WPM | Comfortable | Focus on consistency and accuracy |

## The 15 Tips

### 1. Learn proper touch typing, even if it feels slow

If you type with two or four fingers while looking at the keyboard, your ceiling is low. Touch typing means every finger has its own keys and your eyes stay on the screen. Start with the [home row keys and finger placement](/blog/home-row-keys-touch-typing/). Your speed may drop for a week or two, and that is completely normal. Within a month you will overtake your old speed.

### 2. Fix your posture and setup

Sit straight, feet flat on the floor, elbows at roughly 90 degrees, wrists floating slightly above the keyboard. The screen should be at eye level or just below. Bad posture leads to tired shoulders after ten minutes, and tired hands make mistakes.

### 3. Never look at the keyboard

This is the hardest habit to break. If you must, cover your hands with a light cloth or a piece of cardboard while practising. It feels uncomfortable for a few days, but it forces your fingers to learn the positions.

### 4. Accuracy first, speed later

Speed built on mistakes collapses in the exam. In most typing tests, uncorrected errors reduce your net speed and some exams disqualify you if errors cross the allowed limit. Aim for 95% accuracy or more before you push for speed.

> Trainer's rule: if your accuracy falls below 95% in a session, slow down by 5 WPM until it comes back. Speed will return on its own; bad habits will not leave on their own.

### 5. Practise daily, not occasionally

Thirty minutes every day beats three hours once a week. Muscle memory is built by repetition over many days. Roz thoda-thoda practice karo, weekend par marathon nahi.

### 6. Split your practice session

A good 30-minute routine looks like this:

- 5 minutes: warm-up drills on home row and common letter pairs
- 10 minutes: targeted drills on your weak keys
- 10 minutes: passage typing like the actual exam
- 5 minutes: review your mistakes and retype the tricky words

### 7. Find and fix your weak keys

Most typists have 5-8 keys that slow them down, often numbers, punctuation, or letters like Q, Z, X and P. In Hindi, it is usually half letters, matras and conjuncts. Note the keys you miss most and drill them separately. Structured typing courses break practice into key groups for exactly this reason.

### 8. Type in words, not letters

Beginners read one letter, type one letter. Advanced typists read a whole word or even a phrase, and the fingers type it as a single movement. Practise common words like "the", "and", "of", "government", "department" until they feel like one action.

### 9. Keep a steady rhythm

Burst typing, where you rush easy words and stall on hard ones, creates errors. Try to keep an even rhythm, like a metronome. Even rhythm at 35 WPM gives better results than bursts of 50 with pauses.

### 10. Use the right key for Shift and Space

Use the opposite hand's Shift key: for a capital "A" (left hand), press the right Shift with your right little finger. Use your thumb for the space bar. These small habits save fractions of a second on every sentence.

### 11. Practise on exam-like passages

Government exam passages are usually formal English or Hindi with official vocabulary, numbers and punctuation. Practise on similar text. The [exam-wise typing tests](/exams/) on TypingTestKaro mimic the format and duration of real exams.

### 12. Train for the full duration

The SSC CHSL typing test runs for 10 minutes and the DEST runs for 15. Many students type well for 2 minutes and then fade. Build stamina by regularly doing 10-minute tests and longer sessions.

### 13. Do not use Backspace as a habit

In some exams, backspace is allowed but costs time; in others, the rules on editing are strict. Train yourself to keep going after small errors during practice tests, then review them afterwards. Constant backspacing breaks rhythm and lowers speed.

### 14. Make practice fun sometimes

Typing drills can get boring. Once or twice a week, play typing games like a typing race or word rain. They improve reaction time and keep motivation high without feeling like study.

### 15. Track progress weekly

Record your net WPM and accuracy every Sunday in a notebook or spreadsheet. Seeing the line go up keeps you motivated. If it goes flat for two weeks, change your routine: new passages, new weak-key drills, or a different time of day.

## Extra Tips for Hindi Typists

Hindi typing speed kaise badhaye is a slightly different question because the keyboard itself is different. A few points:

- **Choose one layout and stick to it.** Switching between Mangal Inscript and Krutidev confuses finger memory. Check which layout your target exam uses. Central exams increasingly use Inscript (Unicode), while several state exams still accept Krutidev or Remington GAIL.
- **Master matras early.** Most Hindi errors come from wrong matras and half letters. Drill them separately.
- **Learn the conjunct method of your layout.** In Inscript, half letters are formed using the halant key; in Remington, many conjuncts have their own key combinations.

You can practise Mangal Inscript, Krutidev and Remington GAIL layouts on the [Hindi typing test](/typing-test/hindi/) page.

## A Sample 4-Week Plan

| Week | Focus | Daily routine |
|---|---|---|
| Week 1 | Posture, home row, no looking at keys | 20 min drills, 10 min slow passages |
| Week 2 | All letter rows, common words | 15 min drills, 15 min passages |
| Week 3 | Numbers, punctuation, weak keys | 10 min weak keys, 20 min exam-style passages |
| Week 4 | Stamina and exam simulation | One full 10- or 15-minute test daily, review errors |

## Common Mistakes That Keep Speed Low

- Practising only easy text with no punctuation or numbers
- Chasing gross speed while ignoring net speed
- Taking only 1-minute tests and never building stamina
- Changing keyboards or layouts every few days
- Skipping practice for a week and then trying to catch up in one day

## FAQs

### How long does it take to increase typing speed?

With 30-45 minutes of daily focused practice, most learners gain 10-15 WPM in 6-8 weeks. Complete beginners who are switching to touch typing may need a little longer before the jump shows.

### Typing speed kaise badhaye in Hindi?

Pick one layout that your exam uses, learn it by touch typing, drill matras and half letters separately, and practise on formal Hindi passages every day. Consistency matters more than long sessions.

### Is 30 WPM enough for government exams?

For many typist posts, 30 WPM net is close to the requirement, but exam rules differ. SSC CHSL asks for 35 WPM in English, while RRB NTPC typist posts ask for 30 WPM in English. Aim at least 5-10 WPM above your target to handle exam pressure.

### Should I focus on speed or accuracy first?

Accuracy first. Uncorrected errors reduce net speed and too many errors can make you fail a qualifying test even with good gross speed. Once you are consistently above 95% accuracy, push for speed.

### Do typing games actually help?

Yes, as a supplement. Games improve reaction time and keep you motivated, but they should not replace passage practice, because exams test sustained typing on formal text.
