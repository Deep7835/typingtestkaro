"Sir, mera speed 32 hai, kya yeh theek hai?" I hear some version of this question every week. The answer depends on who you are and what you need the speed for. A school student, a data entry operator and a stenographer have very different targets. This article gives you realistic ranges for the average typing speed and explains what counts as a good typing speed for government jobs in India.

A quick note before we start: there is no single official national survey of typing speeds. The ranges below are practical, trainer-level estimates based on what typing instructors and typing test platforms commonly observe. Treat them as guidance, not as scientific data.

## How Typing Speed Is Measured

Typing speed is measured in WPM (words per minute), where one "word" is counted as 5 keystrokes including spaces. Two numbers matter:

- **Gross WPM:** all keystrokes, including mistakes
- **Net WPM:** gross speed minus a penalty for uncorrected errors

When people compare "average typing speed", they usually mean net or near-net speed on a normal English passage. If you are new to these terms, our explainer on [WPM, net WPM and KDPH](/blog/what-is-wpm-typing-speed/) covers the formulas with examples.

## Average Typing Speed: General Ranges

For most adults who use a computer regularly but have never trained in touch typing, speed tends to sit somewhere in the 30-40 WPM range in English. People who learned touch typing properly and type every day often reach 50-70 WPM. Speeds above 80-90 WPM are uncommon and usually belong to professional typists, transcriptionists, writers and programmers who type for many hours daily.

| Level | Typical net speed (English) | What it usually looks like |
|---|---|---|
| Beginner | Below 20 WPM | Looks at keys, uses few fingers |
| Casual user | 20-35 WPM | Some finger habits, frequent pauses |
| Average regular user | 35-45 WPM | Comfortable, mostly accurate |
| Good / skilled | 45-60 WPM | Touch typist, steady rhythm |
| Fast | 60-80 WPM | Trained, daily heavy use |
| Professional | 80+ WPM | Transcription, data work, competitive typists |

Hindi typing speeds are generally lower than English because of matras, half letters and conjuncts that need extra keystrokes. A Hindi typist at 25-30 WPM is roughly comparable to an English typist at 35-40 WPM in terms of effort.

## Average Typing Speed by Age

Age by itself does not decide speed. Practice does. Still, some patterns are common:

| Age group | What is commonly seen | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Children (8-12) | 10-25 WPM | Hands are still small; focus on correct fingers, not speed |
| Teenagers (13-18) | 20-40 WPM | Phone typing is common, but keyboard speed varies widely |
| Young adults (18-30) | 30-50 WPM | Peak learning period; most exam aspirants are here |
| Adults (30-50) | 30-45 WPM | Depends heavily on job; office workers often faster |
| 50+ | 20-40 WPM | Can still improve steadily with regular practice |

> Age is not a barrier. I have seen aspirants in their 30s go from 15 WPM to 40 WPM in three months simply by switching to touch typing and practising every day.

## Typing Speed by Profession

Some jobs demand more speed than others. Here is a practical view:

- **Students and general office users:** 30-45 WPM is plenty for emails, assignments and reports.
- **Clerks and typists (government and private):** 35-50 WPM in English, or 25-35 WPM in Hindi, is a comfortable working range.
- **Data entry operators:** Speed is often measured in key depressions per hour. For example, SSC's Data Entry Speed Test asks for 8,000 KDPH, which works out to roughly 27 WPM, but with high accuracy on numbers and mixed text.
- **Content writers and journalists:** Often 50-70 WPM because they type all day.
- **Transcriptionists and court typists:** Frequently 70 WPM and above, since they type while listening.
- **Programmers:** Speed varies; accuracy with symbols matters more than raw WPM.

## What Is a Good Typing Speed for Government Jobs?

This is where the question becomes practical. Government typing tests are qualifying in nature, which means you need to meet a minimum speed with acceptable accuracy. Here are the requirements as per recent notifications. Always confirm with the latest official notification for your exam.

| Exam / Post | Required speed | Duration |
|---|---|---|
| SSC CHSL (LDC/JSA) | 35 WPM English or 30 WPM Hindi | 10 minutes |
| SSC CHSL DEO (DEST) | 8,000 key depressions per hour | 15 minutes |
| SSC CGL Tax Assistant (DEST) | 8,000 key depressions per hour | 15 minutes |
| RRB NTPC typist posts | 30 WPM English or 25 WPM Hindi | 10 minutes |
| CPCT (MP) | Commonly 30 NWPM English, 20 NWPM Hindi (varies by post) | 15 minutes each |

You can see full details on the [SSC CHSL](/exams/ssc-chsl/) and [CPCT](/exams/cpct/) exam pages, and the same exam section covers RRB NTPC and the DEST as well.

### The safety margin rule

The minimum speed is not your target. Exam day brings nerves, an unfamiliar keyboard and a passage you have never seen. In my experience, most students type 5-8 WPM slower in the actual exam than at home. So set your practice target like this:

- If the requirement is 35 WPM, aim for 40-45 WPM net at home.
- If the requirement is 30 WPM Hindi, aim for 35 WPM net.
- For DEST, aim to comfortably exceed 2,000 correct keystrokes in 15 minutes.

## Is My Typing Speed Good?

A simple way to judge yourself:

- **Below 25 WPM:** You need structured practice, starting with touch typing basics.
- **25-35 WPM:** You are on track, but not yet safe for most typist posts.
- **35-45 WPM:** Good for most government exams if accuracy is 95% or more.
- **Above 45 WPM:** Excellent for exams; focus on consistency over 10-15 minutes.

Accuracy matters as much as speed. A 45 WPM typist with 88% accuracy may perform worse in an exam than a 38 WPM typist with 97% accuracy, because errors reduce net speed and too many errors can disqualify a candidate.

## How to Move Above Average

If your speed is average or below, the path forward is clear:

1. Learn touch typing with correct finger placement.
2. Practise 30-45 minutes every day.
3. Measure net speed and accuracy weekly.
4. Practise on exam-style passages for the full test duration.

## FAQs

### What is the average typing speed?

For adults who use computers regularly without formal training, typing speed commonly falls in the 30-40 WPM range in English. Trained touch typists usually reach 50 WPM or more.

### Is 40 WPM a good typing speed?

Yes. 40 WPM net with good accuracy is above average for general users and meets the English speed requirement of most government typist posts, including SSC CHSL (35 WPM) and RRB NTPC (30 WPM).

### What is a good typing speed in Hindi?

For exam purposes, 30 WPM net in Hindi is a solid level, and 35 WPM gives you a safety margin. Hindi speeds are naturally lower than English because of matras and conjuncts.

### Can I improve my typing speed after 30?

Absolutely. Typing is a motor skill that responds to practice at any age. With daily touch-typing practice, most adults see clear gains within 6-8 weeks.
