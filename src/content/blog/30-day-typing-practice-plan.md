Most people who want to learn typing do not fail because typing is hard. They fail because their practice has no structure. They type a random test one day, skip three days, try a game, then take another test and feel disappointed that nothing has changed.

A plan fixes that. This 30-day typing practice plan is the same structure I recommend to students preparing for government typing tests. It works for English and for Hindi, whether you use Inscript or Remington GAIL. You need about 45 to 75 minutes a day and a physical keyboard.

> Be honest about your starting point. If you already type 25 WPM with touch typing, skip to week 2. If you still look at the keyboard, start from day 1 even if it feels slow.

## Before you start - set up for success

Take ten minutes on day zero to prepare.

- **Take a baseline test.** Do a 2-minute test on our typing test page and note your WPM and accuracy. This is your starting point.
- **Pick one language and one layout.** If your exam needs both English and Hindi, you can follow this plan for both, but start the second language only after week 1 of the first.
- **Fix your posture.** Chair height so your elbows bend around 90 degrees, screen at eye level, wrists relaxed above the keyboard.
- **Get a notebook.** Write down each day's speed and accuracy. Seeing the trend keeps you motivated.

## The plan at a glance

| Week | Days | Goal | Daily time | Key activities | Target by end of week |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 1-7 | Learn correct finger placement | 45 min | Home row, top row, bottom row drills, no looking at keys | All keys known, 10-15 WPM, 90%+ accuracy |
| 2 | 8-14 | Build accuracy and rhythm | 60 min | Common words, short sentences, 2-5 minute tests | 18-22 WPM, 93%+ accuracy |
| 3 | 15-21 | Build speed and stamina | 60-75 min | 5-10 minute tests, error review, punctuation and numbers | 25-28 WPM, 95%+ accuracy |
| 4 | 22-30 | Exam simulation | 60-75 min | Full-length mocks in exam format, weak-word drills | Exam target speed with 95%+ accuracy |

These targets are typical for a motivated learner practising daily in English. Hindi usually takes a little longer because of matras and conjuncts, so expect somewhat lower numbers in the same time. Everyone progresses at a different pace, and that is normal.

## Week 1 (Days 1-7) - Finger placement

The only goal this week is correct technique. Speed does not matter yet.

### Daily routine

- **10 minutes** - Review keys learned so far.
- **20 minutes** - Learn and drill new keys.
- **10 minutes** - Type simple words using only known keys.
- **5 minutes** - Short untimed paragraph, eyes on screen.

### Day-by-day focus

- **Days 1-2** - Home row. Rest your fingers on A S D F and J K L ; and learn every home row key. Our guide on [home row keys and touch typing](/blog/home-row-keys-touch-typing/) explains exactly where each finger goes.
- **Days 3-4** - Top row. Add a few keys at a time, always returning to the home row.
- **Day 5** - Bottom row.
- **Day 6** - Shift key, capitals and common punctuation (or matras and halant for Hindi).
- **Day 7** - Full keyboard review and a 2-minute test.

If you use a structured course like our [English typing course](/courses/english-typing/), follow its lesson order during this week.

## Week 2 (Days 8-14) - Accuracy and rhythm

Now you know where the keys are. This week you train your fingers to move smoothly between them.

### Daily routine

- **10 minutes** - Warm-up on your weakest keys.
- **15 minutes** - Drill the 100 most common words of your language.
- **15 minutes** - Sentences and short paragraphs.
- **15 minutes** - Three timed tests of 2 to 5 minutes each.
- **5 minutes** - Note your results and weak words.

### Tips for week 2

- Aim for **steady rhythm**, not bursts. Every key should take about the same time.
- If accuracy drops below 92%, slow down deliberately for the next test.
- Short, fun sessions on our [typing games](/games/) on rest days can keep your practice going without feeling like work.

## Week 3 (Days 15-21) - Speed and stamina

This is where real speed gains usually happen. You now practise longer tests and start analysing your errors seriously.

### Daily routine

- **10 minutes** - Warm-up on weak keys and difficult words.
- **10 minutes** - Numbers, punctuation and capital letters (or conjuncts for Hindi).
- **20 minutes** - Two 10-minute timed tests.
- **15 minutes** - Error review. Retype every wrong word five times.
- **5 minutes** - Record results.

### Tips for week 3

- Increase test length gradually - 5 minutes early in the week, 10 minutes by the end.
- Read one or two words ahead of your fingers, not a whole line.
- Keep your hands relaxed. Tension makes you slower and more error-prone.
- Try "burst" practice: type a single sentence as fast as possible three times, then type it once at a comfortable pace. This stretches your top speed without ruining accuracy.

For more techniques, read [how to increase typing speed](/blog/how-to-increase-typing-speed/).

## Week 4 (Days 22-30) - Exam simulation

The final stretch is about performing under exam conditions. By now, you should practise in the exact format of your target exam: same language, same layout, same duration.

### Daily routine

- **10 minutes** - Warm-up.
- **10-15 minutes** - One full-length mock test in exam format.
- **15 minutes** - Error review and weak-word drill.
- **10-15 minutes** - A second mock, or focused drills if you are tired.
- **5 minutes** - Record results.

### Tips for week 4

- Practise at the same time of day as your exam slot if you know it.
- Use the full duration. If your exam is 10 minutes, practise with our [10-minute typing test](/typing-test/10-minute/) rather than shorter tests.
- Take one lighter day (days 26 or 27) to avoid burnout.
- On days 29 and 30, do only one mock and some light drills. Arrive at your exam fresh, not exhausted.

> Track your net speed, not just gross speed. A 35 WPM gross with many errors can drop well below the qualifying mark once mistakes are deducted.

## Adjusting the plan for Hindi typing

The same four-week structure works for Hindi, with a few changes:

- In week 1, spend extra time on matras and the halant (Inscript) or on the pre-typed short i-matra and reph (Remington GAIL).
- In weeks 2 and 3, add a daily 5-minute drill on conjuncts such as क्ष, त्र, ज्ञ, श्र and common words like प्रशासन, क्षेत्र, राष्ट्र.
- Adjust targets downwards. Many state exams typically ask for around 25 WPM in Hindi, so that is a realistic final goal rather than 35.

## What to do if you fall behind

Missing a day or two is normal. Do not try to double up the next day. Simply continue with the next day's plan. If you miss most of a week, repeat that week before moving on. The order matters more than the calendar.

If you reach day 30 and are still below your exam target, do not panic. Repeat week 3 and week 4 once more. Most learners who practise consistently for six weeks reach typical exam speeds.

## FAQs

### Can I become exam-ready in 30 days?

If you practise daily and follow a structure, 30 days is often enough to reach typical exam speeds in English, especially if you already know some typing. Complete beginners, and Hindi learners, may need a few more weeks.

### How many hours a day should I practise typing?

Around 45 to 75 minutes of focused daily practice works better than long, irregular sessions. Short breaks every 20 to 30 minutes help your hands and focus.

### Should I focus on speed or accuracy first?

Accuracy first. Most exams deduct for errors, so aim for 93 to 95% accuracy before pushing for speed. Speed builds naturally on top of accurate technique.

### Can I use this plan for English and Hindi together?

Yes, but start the second language only after you have finished week 1 of the first. Practise them in separate sessions so your fingers do not mix up the layouts.

### What should I do on the day before the exam?

Take one short test to stay warm, check your exam details, and rest well. Heavy practice the day before usually does more harm than good.
