Every fast typist you have ever seen has one thing in common: their fingers know where every key is without looking. That skill is called **touch typing**, and it starts with a single row of the keyboard called the **home row**. If you are preparing for a government typing test, or you simply want to stop hunting for keys, this is the most important lesson you will ever take.

## What Is Touch Typing?

Touch typing is typing without looking at the keyboard, using all ten fingers, with each finger responsible for a fixed set of keys. Your eyes stay on the screen or the passage, and your fingers find keys by position and feel.

Compare that to "hunt and peck" typing, where you look down, find a key and press it with one or two fingers. Hunt-and-peck typists usually hit a ceiling well below what exams demand, because every glance at the keyboard costs time and breaks concentration.

## What Are the Home Row Keys?

The home row is the middle row of letter keys. On a standard English QWERTY keyboard, the home row keys for your fingers are:

- **Left hand:** A, S, D, F
- **Right hand:** J, K, L, ; (semicolon)

Your fingers rest on these eight keys whenever they are not pressing something else. Every other key is reached from here, and your fingers return here after each stroke. The G and H keys sit between the two hands on the home row and are pressed by the index fingers.

### The bumps on F and J

Look closely at your keyboard. The F and J keys have small raised bumps or lines. These let your index fingers find the home position without looking. Whenever you lose your place, touch the bumps and reset.

## Finger Placement Chart

Here is which finger is responsible for which keys on a standard QWERTY keyboard:

| Finger | Left hand keys | Right hand keys |
|---|---|---|
| Little finger | Q, A, Z, 1, Tab, Caps Lock, left Shift | P, ;, /, 0, -, =, [, ], ', Enter, Backspace, right Shift |
| Ring finger | W, S, X, 2 | O, L, ., 9 |
| Middle finger | E, D, C, 3 | I, K, , (comma), 8 |
| Index finger | R, F, V, T, G, B, 4, 5 | U, J, M, Y, H, N, 6, 7 |
| Thumb | Space bar | Space bar |

Notice that the index fingers do the most work: each one covers two columns. The little fingers handle the edge keys, which is why they often feel weak at first.

> Golden rule of touch typing: every finger returns to its home key after each stroke. If you remember only one thing from this article, remember this.

## Correct Posture Before You Start

Finger placement works only when your body is set up correctly:

- Sit upright with your back supported and feet flat on the floor.
- Keep elbows close to your body, bent at about 90 degrees.
- Keep wrists straight and slightly raised; do not rest them heavily on the desk while typing.
- Curve your fingers gently, as if holding a small ball.
- Position the screen at or slightly below eye level, about an arm's length away.
- Keep the keyboard directly in front of you, with the space bar centred on your body.

## Using Shift and Space the Right Way

Two habits separate good typists from average ones:

- **Opposite-hand Shift:** To type a capital letter with the left hand (like "A" or "S"), hold the right Shift with the right little finger. For right-hand letters (like "J" or "K"), use the left Shift.
- **Thumb on space:** Use one thumb consistently for the space bar. Most people use the right thumb, but either is fine as long as you are consistent.

## Beginner Drills for the Home Row

Start slowly. Speed does not matter in the first week; correct fingers do.

### Drill 1: Home row only

Type these lines several times each, without looking down:

- asdf jkl; asdf jkl;
- fdsa ;lkj fdsa ;lkj
- sad lad fad dad
- ask flask falls salad

### Drill 2: Add G and H

- fgf jhj fgf jhj
- has glad half shall
- flash gash hall dash

### Drill 3: Top row reach

Move up from the home row and return immediately:

- frf juj ded kik sws lol
- fur just tree kite rule

### Drill 4: Bottom row reach

- fvf jmj dcd k,k sxs l.l
- came move exam bank

Once these feel comfortable, move on to full words and sentences. The [English typing course](/courses/english-typing/) on TypingTestKaro walks you through every row in a structured order.

## Home Row in Hindi Typing

Hindi layouts also have a home row, but the characters differ:

- **Mangal Inscript:** Inscript places vowels and matras mostly on the left hand and consonants mostly on the right. The index fingers still rest on the F and J key positions. Because Inscript is the Government of India standard for Unicode Hindi typing, it is widely used in central exams. Start with the [Hindi Mangal Inscript course](/courses/hindi-mangal-inscript/).
- **Remington GAIL / Krutidev:** This layout follows the Krutidev 010 font keyboard and is different from Inscript. Many state exams accept it. The same touch-typing principles apply: fixed home keys, return after each stroke, no looking down. The [Hindi Remington GAIL course](/courses/hindi-remington-gail/) covers it step by step.

The physical finger positions are the same in all layouts. Only the characters printed on the keys change, which is why a good English touch typist usually picks up a Hindi layout faster.

## How Long Does It Take to Learn Touch Typing?

With 30 minutes of daily practice, most learners can place all fingers correctly without looking within two to three weeks. Speed usually drops at first when you switch from hunt-and-peck, then climbs past your old speed within a month or so. Reaching exam-level speeds of 30-40 WPM typically takes a few months of steady practice, depending on your starting point.

| Stage | Typical timeline | Focus |
|---|---|---|
| Home row comfort | 3-5 days | Correct fingers, no looking |
| All letter rows | 1-2 weeks | Reaching and returning |
| Numbers and symbols | 2-3 weeks | Top row, Shift keys |
| Full passages | 3-4 weeks onward | Rhythm, accuracy, stamina |

## Common Beginner Mistakes

- Peeking at the keyboard "just once"
- Using the index finger for keys that belong to other fingers
- Letting fingers drift away from the home row
- Pressing keys too hard, which causes fatigue
- Rushing for speed before finger positions are automatic

## FAQs

### What are the home row keys?

On a QWERTY keyboard, the home row keys are A, S, D, F for the left hand and J, K, L, ; for the right hand. Your thumbs rest on the space bar.

### Why do F and J have bumps?

The bumps let your index fingers find the home position by touch, so you can place your hands correctly without looking at the keyboard.

### Is touch typing necessary for government typing tests?

It is not a written rule, but in practice it is very hard to reach 30-35 WPM with good accuracy for 10-15 minutes without touch typing. Almost every successful candidate types by touch.

### Can I learn touch typing if I already type with two fingers?

Yes. Your speed may drop for the first couple of weeks while your fingers adjust, but you will soon overtake your old speed and keep improving.

### Are home row keys the same for Hindi typing?

The finger positions are the same, with index fingers on the F and J positions, but the characters on those keys depend on the layout, such as Mangal Inscript or Remington GAIL.
